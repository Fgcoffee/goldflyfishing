    Inert cards
    
  
    
    Cards the parser could not read. They did nothing all run, so every number
    above understates them.
  
    
  
    

    



    
  
    Swap lab
    
  
    
    Try cards out against each other on the same seed. Two ordinary
    runs cannot answer “is this card worth a slot”: the gap between
    two decks differing by one card is usually smaller than the gap between two
    runs of the same deck, so the noise wins. Every variant here plays the same
    seed against the same opponents, which takes that noise out of the answer.
  
    

  
    
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
  
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
  
    

  
    
  
    

    



    
  
    Cut lab
    
  
    
    Got more cards than slots? Paste the whole list. Lands stay in; every other
    card goes on trial. Phase 1 plays many random hundred-card versions of
    the deck to find the core — cards clearly worth their slot.
    Phase 2 puts the cards nearest the cut line head to head on the same
    seeds, dropping the weakest each round until the deck fits.

      Replay
      
  
      
    One game, played again from its seed. Turns are numbered the way the player
    who took them would number them — the engine counts every player’s
    turn, so a four-player game’s fourth round is its thirteenth turn, and
    nobody means that by “turn four”.
  
      

  
      
    
    
    
    
      
      
      
      
    
      
    
  
      

  
      

  
  
      
    
      
      
      
      
      
      
    
      
    
      
    
      
      Click a card to see it whole. A gold border is a commander, a yellow one a
      card the parser only partly read, and a red one a card it could not read at
      all — those do nothing, however good they look on the board.

      Sandbox
      
  
      
    Put cards where you want them and play them in whatever order you like.
    The opponent does nothing, so anything that happens is your card or the
    rules. Inspect a card first to see what the parser made of it — a
    card that does nothing is either an unread ability or a rules bug, and
    those need different fixes.

        Rules switched off

        the rule itself. Nothing here changes what a card does.
